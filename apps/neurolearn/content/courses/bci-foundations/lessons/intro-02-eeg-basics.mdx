<Hook>
If EEG only measures tiny voltages on the scalp, how do we turn that into a usable brain–computer signal?
</Hook>

## BCIs and where EEG fits

**Brain–computer interfaces (BCIs)** let the brain communicate with an external device. That requires measuring neural activity and a machine that can **filter, amplify, and translate** those signals—often so a person can control something with brain-derived commands.

**EEG** is a common **non-invasive** approach: electrodes on the scalp, no surgery. Non-invasive signals are usually **much weaker** than invasive recordings closer to neurons.

## What EEG actually measures

EEG measures the **difference in electrical potential between scalp sites**. It has **poor spatial localization**, so pinpointing an exact cortical source is hard compared with some other methods.

Electrodes are often **silver / silver chloride (Ag/AgCl)** discs. They help convert **ionic currents** (from the biology of Na/K activity) into **electronic current** the amplifier can read.

**Electrolytic gel** is commonly used to lower **impedance** so more of the tiny signal reaches the electronics.

## Passive and active electrodes

- **Passive electrodes** send relatively raw signals toward a remote amplifier
- **Active electrodes** amplify near the scalp with a tiny built-in circuit, which can improve robustness to noise and cable effects

Systems also use **differential amplification** to reject noise that appears on both inputs (shared environmental interference).

## The 10–20 placement system

Electrode positions are planned with the **10–20 system**: spacing based on **10% or 20%** of skull distances, anchored by landmarks like the **nasion** (nose bridge) and **inion** (bump at the back of the skull).

Letter codes (common teaching set):

- **F** frontal · **C** central · **T** temporal · **P** parietal · **O** occipital

**Odd numbers** mark the **left** hemisphere, **even numbers** the **right**, and **z** (for “zero”) marks the **midline**, as in **Cz** at the top of the head. Common reference sites include the **mastoids** (the bones behind the ears) and **Cz**.

## Amplification matters

Raw EEG is tiny—often about **5–100 µV**—so systems need **large, careful amplification** before digitization and analysis.

<Callout kind="myth">
More electrodes alone does not equal mind-reading. Coverage helps sampling; it does not invent information the scalp signal never contained.
</Callout>
